# Exercise catalog

Read before changing catalog data or exercise drawings. Paths and commands below are relative to `apps/web`.

## Muscles

Every exercise's `primaryMuscles` and `secondaryMuscles` come from `scripts/catalog/muscles.json`, a
reviewed, app-owned table in Ember's 20-muscle vocabulary (`scripts/catalog/muscles.py` and
`muscles` in `src/catalog/catalog.ts`). wger and Everkinetic muscle tags are not
used: they mis-tag some exercises (Hip Adductor as Glutes, Shrug as Shoulders, Leg
Press with Calves as primary). Primary means a prime mover; secondary a
synergist that gets a real stimulus, not a stabiliser. Tag variants consistently.
After editing the table, run `python3 scripts/catalog/muscles.py`;
`scripts/catalog/generate.py` applies it on every full rebuild and refuses a table that doesn't
cover the catalog exactly. Add a new exercise to the table in the same change.

## Body figure

`src/catalog/body-paths.ts` (the Muscles figure) is generated by `scripts/catalog/body_paths.py`
from react-native-body-highlighter (MIT, pinned commit, licence kept in the
generated file). Paths are unchanged; the script only groups them into Ember's
regions (serratus slivers, front calf slivers, tibialis, neck and joints stay plain
body; the tall upper-back wedge is Lats). Usage is in the script's docstring.

## Drawings

- Every catalog exercise has a drawing (`tests/catalog.test.ts`, "gives every exercise a drawing"); a new exercise needs one before it ships.
- Licensed sources first: re-check wger (`/api/v2/exerciseimage/?exercise=<id>`) for new licensed drawings. free-exercise-db has only photos, which the catalog excludes.

The licensed base catalog is built by `scripts/catalog/generate.py` from reviewed open-source
mappings. Do not replace or reattribute those illustrations. The catalog holds
drawings and exercise facts only; it bundles no instruction text.
Everkinetic drawings come from `dist/png`; when an exercise has none there,
`scripts/catalog/generate.py` falls back to the artist's finished source exports
`src/images-ai/<id>-S.ai.png` and `-F.ai.png` ("ai" = Adobe Illustrator, same
licence and attribution). Build from a full Everkinetic checkout, including
`src/`, or those drawings are dropped. Check that an Everkinetic id's title
matches the exercise before mapping it.

Some `dist/png` drawings are cut off at the legs, feet or equipment. For the
exercises in `EK_FULL_FIGURE` (`scripts/catalog/generate.py`), the artist's full-figure exports
are used instead. They are cleaned of stray specks first (`despeck()`). Review each pair side by
side for exercise, pose and start/finish order; some exports label start and
finish backwards, so the mapping lists files in display order.
Keep an exercise on `dist` when its full-figure export is near-identical, already
uncropped or no better: shrugs lose their visible shoulder lift at full-figure
scale.

Dumbbell and cable shrugs are cropped to the upper body (`EK_UPPER_BODY`, the
same box for start and finish, hands and weight fully in frame) so the small shoulder lift stays visible;
barbell and Smith machine shrugs stay whole to keep their plates and rails.
Lying Leg Raise uses Everkinetic's Flat Bench Leg Raises (0021) drawing.
`EK_UNRELEASED` maps exercises Everkinetic doesn't publish to its unpublished
drawings in `src/images-png-unknown` (same artist and licence, numbered, no
title); each pair is checked against the exercise variant and listed in display
order, and its credit is titled by drawing number. Check each pair by eye for the
exact variant (stance, grip, equipment) and frame order: #320 looks like a sumo
deadlift in a contact sheet but is a conventional one (hip-width feet, hands outside
the knees), #618 is a seated knee tuck rather than a sit-up, #630 finishes like a
curl (elbows down) rather than a clean's front rack, and #521 is a hinged
hand-to-hand kettlebell pass with no swing finish.
`EK_NO_DRAWING` skips Everkinetic's drawing where it shows a different variant:
Pull Up (Assisted) shows the approved assisted-machine pair (also used by Chin
Up (Assisted)) through `scripts/catalog/generated.py`.

## Line width

Every drawing, licensed or app-owned, is redrawn from its full-size source by
`scripts/catalog/stroke.py` before the 240px mask is made, so all drawings share one line weight.
It finds each line's centre and drawn width, scales every line's width by one
factor per drawing (outlines stay heavier than interior lines; solid fills keep
their size) and tunes that factor until the mask's mean line width is 2.1px, the
median of the licensed Everkinetic drawings before normalisation. The pose and
every line stay where they were drawn. Mean line width is 2 × ink area ÷ outline
length, the outline measured over four directions (`stroke.stroke_width`).
`tests/catalog.test.ts` holds every bundled drawing to 1.7–2.7px, the licensed
drawings' 5th–95th percentile. A full `scripts/catalog/generate.py` run takes about 10 minutes.
Uniform filters can't do this: `MinFilter` only thickens and merges nearby lines,
`MaxFilter` erases fine interior lines. Thickening closes gaps narrower than the new
line width, so judge dense machines and chains on the normalised mask, not the raw PNG.

AI-assisted drawings also get a hand-drawn line pass (`scripts/catalog/pencil.py`,
`pencil-v1`) inside that redraw, so their lines read like Everkinetic's: width swells and
thins along a line, some free ends taper, a stem thins into the line it meets and at some
T-junctions stops just short of it. The bar of a T is never thinned or cut, at most one gap
opens per junction, small closed parts stay closed, jitter stays within ±15% of the line's width
and paper narrower than 3 mask px is never filled. The tuning loop measures width after the
pass (six rounds), so the 2.1px target and the band test hold. It is seeded from each source
PNG's SHA-256, so reruns give the same bytes. Level `default` applies unless the drawing's
record for `scripts/catalog/generated.py` sets `light`, `strong` or `off`; the manifest
(`scripts/catalog/generated-manifest.json`) lists each `line` and the credit's `changes` note names it. Licensed
drawings never get the pass. It needs numpy (built with numpy 2.5 and Pillow 13; other versions may
shift bytes); `scripts/catalog/generate.py` runs on Pillow alone. Its tests
(`scripts/catalog/test_pencil.py`) run locally, not in CI.

## App-owned illustrations

Drawings made for Ember with AI assistance are always credited as AI-assisted. By default
a drawing is credited to `Ember (AI-assisted, gpt-image-2)` with license `app-owned`, and
its adaptation note says it is AI-assisted and not an Everkinetic or wger work. The
`app-owned` label describes where the drawing came from; it claims neither exclusive
copyright nor a Creative Commons license. A drawing made with Everkinetic drawings of
other exercises as style references is credited to `Ember (AI-assisted, after
Everkinetic)` under Everkinetic's licence, CC BY-SA 4.0, and its adaptation note names
those style references. Licensed Everkinetic and wger drawings keep their original
attribution and terms. `tests/catalog.test.ts` checks these credits.
